# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A single discretionary trader (the project owner). Sits down for a focused
review session — typically after a daily market scan — and triages the screener
output by eye: tier, score, structural tags, and the card mini-chart. The job to
be done is **fast, trustworthy triage** — sort a scan into "worth my attention"
vs "skip" in seconds, then bridge the survivors into TWS / TradingView for deeper
review or a discretionary trade.

Trading style is a Minervini-VCP + Qullamaggie momentum mix read through a
Wyckoff lens; the trader is the decision-maker, never the tool. Beyond the live
screener, the same user reviews the **archive** (historical setups with their
forward outcomes) and a **trade journal**, and increasingly wants to answer one
question: *what edge does this engine actually have?*

## Product Purpose

Chrollo is a Wyckoff/VCP setup screener and personal trading-research lab. It
scans the market for tight-structure bases (LPS / VCP / rebound), scores and
tiers them, archives every signal with its full structural fingerprint, and —
the strategic direction the project is committing to — measures the engine's
**unbiased standalone edge** by tracking forward returns on everything it flags
(not a cherry-picked-winner recall test).

The engine's prime directive is **accurate visual tight-structure detection**.
Success is two-fold: (1) the trader trusts the screener as a daily idea source,
and (2) the trader can quantify the edge it actually carries — which winners it
surfaces, including ones they'd never have considered on their own.

Chrollo is explicitly **not** an autonomous trader, **not** a brokerage, and
**not** a TradingView clone. Broker integration (IBKR) is read-only portfolio
context. It is an instrument for one trader's judgment, not a replacement for it.

## Brand Personality

**Calm. Precise. Decisive.**

A calm instrument with a sharp point of view. The surface stays quiet — you stare
at it for hours, so it never shouts, celebrates, or competes with the data — but
the hierarchy is confident: every screen commits to what matters most rather than
laying everything out flat and equal. Quiet chrome, decisive signal.

Voice in UI copy is plain and specific: say what a control does and what a number
means, with no hype, no marketing cadence, no exclamation. The emotional target
is *focused confidence* — the trader feels in control and unhurried.

## Anti-references

- **Gamified retail broker (Robinhood et al.).** No dopamine mechanics: no
  confetti, no celebratory animation on a win, no oversized green P&L designed to
  feel good. A research lab must never bias the trader's read of its own output.
- **Generic SaaS dashboard.** No endless identical card grids, no gradient
  hero-metric tiles, no tiny uppercase eyebrow kickers above every section. The
  default AI-dashboard look is a failure state here.
- **Over-designed.** No decorative glassmorphism, no heavy or showy motion, no
  ornamental gradients that sit between the trader and the data. Restraint is the
  brand.

## Design Principles

1. **Signal over chrome.** The data is the product. UI recedes so structure,
   score, and tags read instantly; decoration that doesn't aid the read is debt.
2. **Confident hierarchy.** Commit to what matters most on every screen. Flat,
   egalitarian layouts where everything competes are a defect, not neutrality.
3. **Honest instrument, never a salesman.** The interface must not nudge,
   celebrate, or inflate. It reports; the trader decides. This mirrors the
   engine-validation north star: measure the truth, don't flatter it.
4. **Earn every element.** Be dense where density informs (a scan grid, a stats
   strip) and empty where it doesn't. Resist the card-grid and hero-metric
   reflexes; reach for the structure the content actually needs.
5. **Built for long sessions.** Optimize for hours of review: comfortable
   contrast, calm color, motion that's optional and never load-bearing. Attention
   is the scarce resource the design protects.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Single known user; no formal WCAG target. Hold body text to reasonable contrast
(≥4.5:1) so long sessions don't strain, and keep motion non-essential as a matter
of hygiene. Win/loss/status currently lean on red/green hue; acceptable for the
current sole user, but if Chrollo ever gains other users, pair hue with shape or
text rather than relying on color alone.
